02 Dataset overview

Music & Mental Health survey

Survey responses about how people listen to music (when, where, how long and which genres) and whether they say music improves their mood, has no effect or makes it worse.

Source: MXMH Music & Mental Health Survey by Catherine Rasgaitis (Kaggle). The rest of the dataset was gathered from supplementary survey responses and two research papers: Music Therapy Practices and Perspectives Regarding the Mental Health Needs of People With Intellectual Disabilities (2026) and Clinical Implications of College Students’ Music Listening Habits and Perceptions of Personal Impact (2025). I chose it because music shapes how people cope and connect, and I wanted to see why it helps some listeners and not others.

03 Problem definition

What shapes the effect music has on people?

travel_exploreThe aim

Describe who the listeners are and how they listen, then compare those habits across the three reported effects: Improve, No effect and Worsen.

lightbulbWhat I expected

Common listening habits, clear differences between groups, a few unusual values worth checking, and variables worth a closer look. These are associations to explore, not causes to prove.

06 Final insights

What I found

72%Music mostly helps

720 of 1,000 say music improves how they feel; 17% feel no effect and 11% feel worse.

−0.74Younger people listen more

Strong negative correlation between age and daily hours: 6.8 h a day at 13–20 against about 2.3 h from 40 up.

85% vs 70%Genre matters most

Ambient, Classical and Lofi listeners report Improve about 85% of the time; Blues and Indie listeners mostly report Worsen.

50%Late night, at home

Half listen mostly late at night and 42% at home. Hours are almost identical across the three effects (4.8–5.0 h).

97% vs 62%Musicians go multilingual

Instrumentalists listen to foreign-language music far more often than non-instrumentalists.

91% vs 22%Groups react differently

Autism (ASD) reports Improve 91% of the time, Bipolar disorder only 22% (mostly No effect), and Depression splits evenly between Improve and Worsen.

140 vs 70Each group has its own sound

ADHD listens to the fastest music (median 140 BPM, EDM and Hip-hop), Anxiety the slowest (70 BPM, Ambient, Classical, Lofi). 90% of ADHD, Autism and OCD replay songs often.

87Outliers kept

Ages above 40.5 (and 2 tempos above about 155 BPM) are flagged by the IQR rule but are real answers, so they stay.

07 Conclusion

What I take away

schoolWhat I learned

  • Each chart answers a different question: shape, counts, relationships or combinations.
  • Group size matters: a big group like Anxiety (360) dominates raw counts, so I compared percentages.
  • Building the dashboard turned the analysis into something people can explore themselves.

warningLimitations

  • An extended version of the survey; some fields are derived or simulated, and mental_disorder is not a diagnosis.
  • The effect is self-reported, and the results show associations, not causes.
  • A young sample (median 21) and several groups under 30 records.

rocket_launchNext steps

  • Repeat the analysis on the original, fully documented survey.
  • Collect more answers for the small genres and groups.
  • For prediction, use stratified cross-validation and per-class results.
